Family movie night is one of those small rituals that quietly holds a household together. A bowl of popcorn, the lights down low, the couch full, and ninety minutes where everyone in the room is laughing at the same joke or quiet at the same scene. The only hard part is picking the movie — and in 2026, Netflix's family catalog is bigger, deeper, and more uneven than it has ever been.

This guide is based on a comprehensive review of reception across major review aggregators, streaming audience data, and viewer feedback — not a single person's opinion, but a balanced summary of what actual viewers report. The goal is simple: to help parents, grandparents, babysitters, and tweens scrolling on their own pick something that will actually land, without wasting twenty minutes arguing over the remote.

The list below covers around thirty films, sorted into the categories families search for most: animated blockbusters, live-action adventures, picks that work best for little kids, picks that work better for tweens and teens, holiday staples, and hidden gems that tend to slip past the algorithm. At the bottom there's a Stream It Together table and an FAQ that answers the questions Google users ask most often about Netflix family viewing.

One caveat to start with: Netflix's catalog rotates constantly. A few beloved titles — including the *How to Train Your Dragon* films, *The Addams Family*, *The Lorax*, and *Daddy Day Care* — are scheduled to leave Netflix across the spring of 2026, so families who want to revisit them should check availability before planning a movie night. Those titles are flagged throughout this guide where relevant.

## Best Animated Family Films on Netflix

Animation is where Netflix's family lineup is strongest in 2026. The platform now hosts a mix of its own originals, licensed DreamWorks and Illumination hits, and a growing shelf of Sony Pictures Animation titles that consistently outperform their box office numbers on streaming.

### The Wild Robot

- **Rating:** PG
- **Runtime:** 1h 42min
- **Best for:** Ages 6+

A robot washes ashore on a remote island after a shipping accident and slowly learns to survive among wild animals — until she ends up as the accidental parent of an orphaned gosling. Adapted from Peter Brown's bestselling book, *The Wild Robot* was one of the most acclaimed animated films of 2024 and arrived on Netflix in 2025, where it has stayed near the top of the kids and family charts. Viewers repeatedly describe it as the rare family movie that makes adults cry harder than the kids. The painterly animation style and gentle pacing make it a standout, and for families wanting something with emotional weight without being scary or sad, this is the consensus top pick.

### The Mitchells vs. the Machines

- **Rating:** PG
- **Runtime:** 1h 54min
- **Best for:** Ages 7+

A quirky, dysfunctional family road trip becomes the last hope for humanity when every smart device on Earth decides to revolt. The Mitchells are an awkward, funny, deeply recognizable family, and the animation style — a mix of 2D doodles layered over 3D — is endlessly inventive. It is widely considered one of the best animated films of the last decade, with near-universal praise from audiences for its humor, heart, and surprisingly honest take on parents and teenagers who do not always understand each other.

### KPop Demon Hunters

- **Rating:** PG
- **Runtime:** 1h 39min
- **Best for:** Ages 8+

A Netflix original that became one of the platform's biggest animated hits of 2024 and has only grown in popularity since. A K-pop girl group moonlights as demon hunters, protecting their fans from supernatural threats between sold-out shows. The soundtrack is genuinely catchy enough to survive repeat viewings, and the action sequences play like anime set pieces crossed with music videos. Families with tweens and teens who are into K-pop culture consistently rank this as their favorite Netflix original of the last two years.

### The Bad Guys

- **Rating:** PG
- **Runtime:** 1h 40min
- **Best for:** Ages 6+

A crew of notorious animal criminals — a wolf, a snake, a piranha, a shark, and a tarantula — try to fake going straight, then accidentally start to mean it. Bright, fast, and funnier than most adult comedies, *The Bad Guys* is a reliable crowd-pleaser for mixed-age audiences. Parents appreciate that the humor never punches down, and younger kids love how cool the heist sequences look.

### The Bad Guys 2

- **Rating:** PG
- **Runtime:** 1h 44min
- **Best for:** Ages 6+

The reformed Bad Guys get pulled back in for one more score in a sequel that doubles down on everything that worked about the first one. Viewers broadly agree the action is bigger, the jokes hit harder, and the animation is even more stylish. Good for back-to-back movie nights with the original.

### Nimona

- **Rating:** PG
- **Runtime:** 1h 39min
- **Best for:** Ages 9+

A disgraced knight and a shape-shifting troublemaker team up to clear his name in a magical medieval-futurist kingdom. Based on ND Stevenson's graphic novel, *Nimona* was nearly lost after its original studio shut down and was rescued by Annapurna before landing on Netflix — and audiences rewarded the journey, making it one of the platform's most-discussed originals. Its themes of identity and acceptance land hardest with tweens and older viewers.

### The Sea Beast

- **Rating:** PG
- **Runtime:** 1h 55min
- **Best for:** Ages 7+

A young orphan stows away on a legendary monster-hunting ship and ends up questioning everything she has been taught about the creatures her world fears. Sweeping, beautifully animated, and surprisingly substantial for a family film, *The Sea Beast* earned an Oscar nomination and remains one of Netflix's better-reviewed originals.

### Orion and the Dark

- **Rating:** TV-Y7
- **Runtime:** 1h 31min
- **Best for:** Ages 6+

Written by Charlie Kaufman of all people, this adaptation of Emma Yarlett's picture book follows a boy terrified of everything — especially the dark — who is visited by the literal embodiment of Night. Thoughtful, funny, and a little strange in the best way, it has become a go-to for families with anxious kids who need a gentle nudge about facing fears.

### Leo

- **Rating:** PG
- **Runtime:** 1h 42min
- **Best for:** Ages 7+

Adam Sandler voices a 74-year-old classroom lizard who, learning his time is running short, tries to escape — and ends up becoming an unlikely therapist to the fifth graders he spent decades watching. Surprisingly sweet, with musical numbers that kids will replay for weeks. Parents often mention it as the rare Sandler comedy they do not mind putting on repeat.

### Vivo

- **Rating:** PG
- **Runtime:** 1h 35min
- **Best for:** Ages 5+

A music-loving kinkajou travels from Havana to Miami to deliver a love letter, powered by original songs from Lin-Manuel Miranda. Vibrant, warm, and frequently overlooked, *Vivo* is one of the most underrated family musicals on Netflix.

### Minions: The Rise of Gru

- **Rating:** PG
- **Runtime:** 1h 27min
- **Best for:** Ages 5+

A young Gru teams up with his yellow henchmen in 1970s San Francisco to join the world's greatest supervillain team. It is loud, silly, and exactly what younger kids want on a Friday night. Parents who remember the earlier films will find enough visual jokes aimed at adults to stay entertained.

### Wish Dragon

- **Rating:** PG
- **Runtime:** 1h 38min
- **Best for:** Ages 7+

A modern Shanghai riff on the wish-granting-spirit story, produced by the team behind *Shrek*. Funny, specific, and one of the better gateway films into animated features set outside the usual Western settings.

### Mr. Peabody & Sherman

- **Rating:** PG
- **Runtime:** 1h 32min
- **Best for:** Ages 6+

A genius dog and his adopted boy travel through history in a time machine, causing just enough chaos to be entertaining. Reviewers consistently list this among the most underrated animated family films on Netflix, especially for kids who are starting to get into history.

## Best Live-Action Family Films on Netflix

Netflix's live-action family catalog is thinner than its animated shelf, but the hits are genuine. Most of the picks here are Netflix originals; the platform has leaned heavily into producing its own live-action family content after its licensing relationships with the traditional studios tightened.

### Enola Holmes

- **Rating:** PG-13
- **Runtime:** 2h 3min
- **Best for:** Ages 10+

Millie Bobby Brown stars as Sherlock Holmes's younger, sharper, and much more rebellious sister, who runs away from home to find her missing mother and stumbles into a much bigger mystery. Smart, fast, and frequently hilarious, with Enola breaking the fourth wall constantly to keep the pace up. Widely considered one of the best family-friendly live-action originals Netflix has produced.

### Enola Holmes 2

- **Rating:** PG-13
- **Runtime:** 2h 9min
- **Best for:** Ages 10+

The sequel is arguably even better, loosely based on the real-life matchgirls' strike of 1888 and adding Henry Cavill's Sherlock into the action more fully. Older tweens and teens consistently rank these as their favorite Netflix originals.

### Yes Day

- **Rating:** PG
- **Runtime:** 1h 26min
- **Best for:** Ages 6+

Two exhausted parents, desperate to reconnect with their kids, agree to say yes to every request for twenty-four hours. Jennifer Garner carries the film with a kind of earnest, stunt-ready charm that makes it a regular rewatch for families with younger kids. Light, short, and easy — the kind of movie you put on when no one wants to commit to something heavy.

### Wonder

- **Rating:** PG
- **Runtime:** 1h 53min
- **Best for:** Ages 8+

Based on R.J. Palacio's bestselling novel, *Wonder* follows Auggie, a fifth grader with facial differences starting mainstream school for the first time. The film does not sugarcoat cruelty from classmates or the fears of parents, but it lands on hope. Many families describe it as the gold standard for a modern family drama — emotionally substantial without being crushing.

### Matilda the Musical

- **Rating:** PG
- **Runtime:** 1h 57min
- **Best for:** Ages 7+

The movie adaptation of the stage musical based on Roald Dahl's book. Bigger, brighter, and more physical than the 1996 version, with song and dance numbers that older kids in particular tend to love. Keeps enough of Dahl's signature darkness to stay interesting for adults.

### The Adam Project

- **Rating:** PG-13
- **Runtime:** 1h 46min
- **Best for:** Ages 10+

Ryan Reynolds plays a time-traveling pilot who crash-lands in 2022 and has to team up with his 12-year-old self. Part action, part father-son story, part sci-fi comedy — it moves fast, and the chemistry between the two Adams does most of the heavy lifting. Older tweens and teens tend to love it.

### We Can Be Heroes

- **Rating:** PG
- **Runtime:** 1h 40min
- **Best for:** Ages 7+

Robert Rodriguez's superhero-kids ensemble, in the lineage of *Spy Kids* and *Sharkboy and Lavagirl*. When the adult Heroics are captured by alien invaders, their kids have to figure out how to save the world. Loud, colorful, and built for younger viewers who want to feel like the hero.

### A Boy Called Christmas

- **Rating:** PG
- **Runtime:** 1h 46min
- **Best for:** Ages 8+

A surprisingly lovely origin story for Santa Claus, based on Matt Haig's novel. Gentle enough for younger kids but real enough to keep parents engaged, with a cast that includes Maggie Smith, Sally Hawkins, and Henry Lawfull in the title role.

### The Boy Who Harnessed the Wind

- **Rating:** TV-PG
- **Runtime:** 1h 53min
- **Best for:** Ages 10+

Based on the true story of William Kamkwamba, a Malawian teenager who built a windmill from scrap parts to save his village from famine. A quiet, inspiring film that works especially well as a conversation starter about resourcefulness, education, and what kids can actually accomplish.

## Best Kids-Specific Picks on Netflix (Ages 4-8)

Not every family movie works for the youngest viewers. The films below are picked for short runtimes, low stakes, bright colors, simple emotional arcs, and minimal scary or sad beats. They are the ones you can leave on while the four-year-old is also playing with Legos in the living room.

### Trolls

- **Rating:** PG
- **Runtime:** 1h 32min
- **Best for:** Ages 4+

Bright, musical, and built on a soundtrack of pop covers that parents will also recognize. Princess Poppy leads her troll friends on a rescue mission in a film designed to be watched and danced to in equal measure.

### Madagascar

- **Rating:** PG
- **Runtime:** 1h 26min
- **Best for:** Ages 4+

Four pampered zoo animals end up shipwrecked on the island of Madagascar in a fast-paced, slapstick-heavy DreamWorks classic. The full trilogy is available on Netflix in 2026, along with the *Penguins of Madagascar* spinoff, making it one of the easiest binges for the preschool-to-early-elementary set.

### The Sea Beast

See above — while it works across the whole family, the monster action is tuned to thrill rather than scare, making it safe for the 5 to 8 range as well.

### Secret Magic Control Agency

- **Rating:** TV-Y7
- **Runtime:** 1h 35min
- **Best for:** Ages 5+

A charming riff on classic fairy tales, following the Hansel and Gretel siblings who have grown up into bickering secret agents. Families who love fairy-tale twists tend to rate this well above its reputation.

### Over the Moon

- **Rating:** PG
- **Runtime:** 1h 40min
- **Best for:** Ages 6+

A young Chinese girl builds a rocket to the moon to find the legendary goddess Chang'e. Gorgeous animation, a strong soundtrack, and genuine emotional weight about grief, told in a way younger kids can absorb safely.

### Leo

See above — its gentle humor and school-based setting work especially well with elementary-aged kids.

## Best Tween and Teen Picks on Netflix (Ages 9-14)

Tween and teen family nights are their own category: something with a little more edge, a little more identity, but still something the whole room can watch without an awkward scene five minutes in. The picks below hit that balance.

### Enola Holmes 1 and 2

See above — a consistent top choice for families with tweens and young teens.

### The Adam Project

See above — the mix of action, comedy, and emotional core is ideal for this age bracket.

### Nimona

See above — its themes of identity land hardest with tweens and teens.

### KPop Demon Hunters

See above — especially strong with tweens already immersed in K-pop culture.

### Spy Kids: Armageddon

- **Rating:** PG
- **Runtime:** 1h 34min
- **Best for:** Ages 9+

A reboot of the beloved franchise, with Robert Rodriguez back in the director's chair. It leans even more into video-game aesthetics than the originals, which is both its weakness with older viewers and its superpower with kids who have grown up inside games.

### Wonder

See above — the film plays strongly with middle schoolers who are navigating their own version of the same social dynamics.

### Rebel Ridge

- **Rating:** R
- **Runtime:** 2h 11min
- **Best for:** Older teens 15+ (parental discretion)

Worth flagging for families with older teens: Jeremy Saulnier's sharp, tightly controlled thriller is one of Netflix's most-praised originals of recent years. It earns its R rating for intensity and some violence rather than sex or crude content, and many families with older teens watch it together as a conversation starter about justice, race, and police power. Not a little-kid pick, but often cited as one of the rare Netflix originals that works for adults and older teens as a shared watch.

## Best Holiday and Seasonal Family Movies on Netflix

Holiday movies have their own logic: they need to feel warm, a little corny, and perfectly willing to coast on sentiment. Netflix has invested heavily in original Christmas family content since 2018, and the best of it holds up to rewatching.

### A Boy Called Christmas

See above — the strongest holiday family film on Netflix and one of the better Santa origin stories ever made.

### The Christmas Chronicles

- **Rating:** PG
- **Runtime:** 1h 44min
- **Best for:** Ages 7+

Kurt Russell plays Santa Claus and absolutely commits, delivering a performance that single-handedly made this one of Netflix's biggest-ever holiday originals. Two siblings try to catch Santa on video and accidentally help him save Christmas. Cheesy in all the right ways.

### The Christmas Chronicles 2

- **Rating:** PG
- **Runtime:** 1h 52min
- **Best for:** Ages 7+

Goldie Hawn joins Russell as Mrs. Claus in a sequel that goes bigger, weirder, and more musical. Some families prefer it to the original.

### Klaus

- **Rating:** PG
- **Runtime:** 1h 36min
- **Best for:** Ages 7+

Probably the best family film Netflix has ever produced. A hand-animated origin story for Santa Claus about a selfish postman and a reclusive toymaker who slowly change a bitter village. Oscar-nominated, gorgeous, and genuinely moving without ever becoming saccharine. The consensus holiday pick.

### Jingle Jangle: A Christmas Journey

- **Rating:** PG
- **Runtime:** 2h 2min
- **Best for:** Ages 7+

A lavish, musical holiday fantasy about a toymaker, his granddaughter, and a magical invention. Forest Whitaker leads a genuinely excellent cast. One of the most ambitious original holiday films Netflix has produced, and a welcome change from the usual rom-com heavy December lineup.

### Christmas on the Square

- **Rating:** TV-PG
- **Runtime:** 1h 38min
- **Best for:** Ages 8+

Dolly Parton plays an angel sent to save a small town from a wealthy landowner's redevelopment plans. Corny, musical, and profoundly committed to its own charm.

### Wendell & Wild

- **Rating:** PG-13
- **Runtime:** 1h 45min
- **Best for:** Ages 12+

Henry Selick, the director of *The Nightmare Before Christmas* and *Coraline*, teams up with Jordan Peele for a stop-motion film about two demon brothers and the teenage girl they team up with. Visually stunning, genuinely weird, and perfect for older tweens and teens who are tired of softer animation.

### My Father's Dragon

- **Rating:** PG
- **Runtime:** 1h 39min
- **Best for:** Ages 6+

A 2D-animated adaptation of Ruth Stiles Gannett's Newbery Honor book, about a young boy who runs away to rescue a dragon. Quiet, imaginative, and a nice change of pace from the platform's louder, faster animated fare.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the best kids movies on Netflix in 2026?

The consensus top tier for 2026 includes *The Wild Robot*, *The Mitchells vs. the Machines*, *KPop Demon Hunters*, *The Bad Guys*, *The Bad Guys 2*, *Leo*, *Orion and the Dark*, *The Sea Beast*, and *Minions: The Rise of Gru*. Across streaming audience data and review aggregators, these titles consistently score high with both kids and parents, and all are available on Netflix as of April 2026.

### Is How to Train Your Dragon on Netflix in 2026?

The *How to Train Your Dragon* films are flagged to leave Netflix in May 2026 as part of a broader rotation of legacy titles. Families who want to revisit them should do so quickly. For similar dragon-centered adventure, *My Father's Dragon* and *The Sea Beast* remain on the platform and fill a similar emotional register for kids in the 6 to 10 range.

### What are the best Netflix movies for family movie night?

For a balanced, whole-family movie night, the safest universal picks are *The Wild Robot*, *The Mitchells vs. the Machines*, *The Bad Guys*, and *Klaus* during the holidays. These are the titles that consistently work across a room containing a five-year-old, a twelve-year-old, and two tired parents. *Yes Day* is a good low-commitment option when nobody wants to sit still for two hours.

### What are the best PG movies on Netflix in 2026?

Within PG-rated family films, the strongest options on Netflix in 2026 include *The Wild Robot*, *The Mitchells vs. the Machines*, *Nimona*, *The Sea Beast*, *Klaus*, *The Christmas Chronicles*, *Leo*, *Wonder*, *Matilda the Musical*, *The Bad Guys*, and *Vivo*. For PG-13 titles that still work for family viewing with older kids, *Enola Holmes*, *Enola Holmes 2*, and *The Adam Project* are the clearest picks.

### What are the best Netflix movies for young children?

For kids roughly ages 4 to 8, the safest, most consistently engaging options are *Trolls*, the *Madagascar* trilogy, *Leo*, *The Bad Guys*, *Orion and the Dark*, *Vivo*, *Over the Moon*, *Secret Magic Control Agency*, and *Minions: The Rise of Gru*. All have short-to-moderate runtimes, low-scare factor, and emotional arcs that younger viewers can follow without adult explanation.

### What Netflix family movies are good for weekends?

Weekend family movies tend to reward slightly longer runtimes and more ambitious stories. The strongest weekend picks on Netflix in 2026 are *The Wild Robot*, *The Mitchells vs. the Machines*, *The Sea Beast*, *Nimona*, *Enola Holmes*, *The Adam Project*, and *Matilda the Musical*. For a back-to-back movie night, *The Bad Guys* and *The Bad Guys 2* are a natural pairing.

### Is The Wild Robot on Netflix?

Yes. *The Wild Robot* arrived on Netflix in the United States in 2025 and has remained on the platform throughout 2026, where it regularly appears on the daily kids and family top ten lists.

### Are there good Netflix family movies for ages 9 to 14?

For tweens and younger teens, the strongest Netflix picks are *Enola Holmes*, *Enola Holmes 2*, *Nimona*, *KPop Demon Hunters*, *The Adam Project*, *Wonder*, *The Boy Who Harnessed the Wind*, *Wendell & Wild* (with parental discretion on the PG-13), and *The Mitchells vs. the Machines*. These films carry enough complexity to hold a tween's attention without leaning into content that is inappropriate for the age range.
