Streaming is the new cable, and as of 2026 most American households subscribe to at least three services. Between Netflix, Max (HBO), Disney+, Hulu, Prime Video, Peacock, Paramount+, Apple TV+, and a growing stable of niche services, picking the right combination for your household has become an annual exercise in reading reviews, comparing bundles, and occasionally cancelling.

This section aggregates our platform-specific guides, genre roundups, and service-by-service comparisons. The goal is straightforward — help readers pick the two or three services that actually match what they want to watch, and skip the rest.

## What Streaming Costs in 2026

Every major service operates on a three-tier model that emerged in 2023–2025: an ad-supported cheap tier, a mid ad-free tier, and a premium tier with 4K and extras. The industry's growth is in ad-supported — families priced out of the premium tiers have defaulted to cheaper tiers with manageable ad loads.

Flat-rate pricing for major services as of April 2026:

- **Netflix**: $7.99 (Ads) / $17.99 (Standard) / $24.99 (Premium)
- **Max (HBO Max)**: $9.99 (Ads) / $16.99 (Ad-Free) / $20.99 (Ultimate)
- **Disney+**: $9.99 (Ads) / $16.99 (No Ads) / $18.99 (Premium 4K)
- **Hulu**: $9.99 (Ads) / $18.99 (No Ads) — Disney+ bundle at $12.99 is the better value
- **Prime Video**: Included with Amazon Prime ($14.99/mo) — $2.99/mo to remove ads
- **Peacock**: $7.99 / $13.99 — Premier League soccer is the kicker
- **Paramount+**: $7.99 (Essential) / $12.99 (P+ with Showtime)
- **Apple TV+**: $9.99 — no ad-supported tier, smallest library, highest quality per title

## The Best Bundles

The **Disney+/Hulu (With Ads) bundle at $12.99/mo** remains the strongest overall value. Adding Max with Ads for another $10/mo brings three full libraries for less than two ad-free standalone subscriptions. For sports households, **Disney+/Hulu/ESPN+ at $19.99/mo** covers a huge share of televised sports plus general entertainment.
