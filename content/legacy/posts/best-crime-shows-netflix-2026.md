Netflix remains the undisputed heavyweight of crime television in 2026, and the slate this year has been one of the strongest in the platform's history. From Joel Kinnaman brooding through a Norwegian serial killer case to Tessa Thompson anchoring a twisty six-episode thriller that hit number one in its first week, the 2026 lineup has delivered for every flavor of crime fan — gritty Nordic noir, true crime docuseries, cartel epics, polished whodunits, and prestige anthologies.

This guide is based on a comprehensive review of reception across major review aggregators, streaming audience data, and viewer feedback — not a single person's opinion, but a balanced summary of what actual viewers report. The shows below are grouped from newest 2026 releases at the top, through established modern hits, international standouts, true crime docuseries, and all-time classics that still hold up. Runtimes, episode counts, and reception notes are included so readers can pick by time commitment as easily as by mood.

A quick note on scope: Netflix rotates its library constantly, and availability can shift by region. Everything below was streaming on Netflix as of April 2026 in most territories, and where a show is still releasing new episodes (like upcoming Season 2 renewals), that status is flagged.

## Best New Crime Series on Netflix (2026)

### His & Hers

- **Episodes:** 6 (limited series) | **Premiered:** January 8, 2026
- **Rotten Tomatoes:** 71% critics (Certified Fresh) | **IMDb:** 7.2/10

Tessa Thompson and Jon Bernthal lead this adaptation of Alice Feeney's bestselling novel about a reclusive London news anchor who returns to her hometown when a childhood friend is murdered — only to find the detective assigned to the case is someone from her past, and that she may be a suspect herself. Viewers report that it gets noticeably better with each episode, and critics praised its slippery unreliable-narrator structure. The series hit number one on Netflix's US top ten in its debut week, and five weeks in it was still drawing more than six million views per week — unusual staying power for a limited thriller. Perfect for fans of *Sharp Objects* or *The Perfect Couple*.

**Who it's for:** Readers of Alice Feeney, Harlan Coben, or Gillian Flynn.

### Jo Nesbø's Detective Hole

- **Episodes:** 9 | **Premiered:** March 20, 2026
- **Based on:** Jo Nesbø's *The Devil's Star* (fifth Harry Hole novel)

After decades of failed film adaptations, Harry Hole finally gets the long-form Nordic noir treatment he deserves. Tobias Santelmann stars as the troubled Oslo detective, with Joel Kinnaman as his menacing corrupt colleague Tom Waaler. The series tracks a serial killer leaving tiny red diamonds at each crime scene while Harry wrestles with alcoholism, grief, and an internal-affairs investigation that's closing in on the wrong man. Reviewers have called it moodier and more successful than the 2017 film *The Snowman*, if slightly overstuffed at nine episodes. Jo Nesbø himself adapted the scripts and showruns, which fans of the books have noticed in the dialogue.

**Why it stands out:** The rare detective adaptation where the author is in the writers' room.

### Peaky Blinders: The Immortal Man

- **Runtime:** Feature film (approx. 2h 10m) | **Released on Netflix:** March 20, 2026
- **Rotten Tomatoes:** 90% critics | **IMDb:** 7.7/10

Not a series, but too important to leave off. Four years after the TV finale, Cillian Murphy returns as Tommy Shelby in a feature-length continuation directed by Tom Harper and written by Steven Knight. When Tommy's estranged son gets pulled into a Nazi-backed plot in the final days before World War II, the old gang reassembles in Birmingham for one last reckoning. It pulled 25.3 million views in its first three days on Netflix — the most-watched title on the platform that week. A satisfying cap on one of the decade's defining crime sagas.

### That Night

- **Episodes:** 6 (limited series) | **Premiered:** March 13, 2026
- **Based on:** Gillian McAllister's novel

A Spanish-language psychological thriller about three adult sisters on a family holiday in the Dominican Republic whose lives unravel after a late-night hit-and-run. The show splits its tension between the cover-up, the siblings' fraying trust, and the slow-motion collapse of the alibi. It became a global top-ten smash within days of release — proof that Netflix's international thriller pipeline is still its most reliable engine. Tight, propulsive, and only six episodes.

**Who it's for:** Fans of *The Perfect Couple* and *Big Little Lies* who want something more claustrophobic.

### Trust Me: The False Prophet

- **Episodes:** 4 (docuseries) | **Premiered:** April 3, 2026
- **Rotten Tomatoes:** 100% critics

A four-part true crime docuseries in which cult experts infiltrate a polygamist offshoot of the FLDS church to expose Samuel Bateman, the self-proclaimed heir to Warren Jeffs. Viewers report being unable to stop watching once the hidden-camera footage starts — critics called it one of the tightest, best-paced cult documentaries Netflix has put out. Compact, specific, and devastating.

### Big Mistakes

- **Episodes:** 8 | **Premiered:** April 2026
- **Genre:** Crime comedy

Dan Levy (*Schitt's Creek*) and Rachel Sennott (*Bottoms*) co-created this tonally weird, very funny crime comedy about two siblings blackmailed into running errands for a suburban organized crime operation. Think *Barry* with a brattier sense of humor. Laurie Metcalf is a standout as the matriarch pulling the strings. A welcome palate cleanser if the true crime docuseries have started blurring together.

### Man on Fire

- **Episodes:** 8 | **Premiered:** April 2026
- **Based on:** A.J. Quinnell's novel series

Yahya Abdul-Mateen II leads this series reimagining of the Man on Fire property, previously a Denzel Washington vehicle in 2004. Abdul-Mateen plays John Creasy, a former Special Forces mercenary struggling with PTSD who takes a bodyguard job that rapidly spirals into a much larger conspiracy. More slow-burn and character-driven than the film, with room to actually sit with Creasy's trauma.

### Agatha Christie's Seven Dials

- **Episodes:** 4 | **Premiered:** January 15, 2026

A plush 1920s whodunit set at a country-house party where an elaborate practical joke ends in a real corpse. Lady Eileen "Bundle" Brent becomes the unlikely investigator, and the series leans into every delicious convention of the genre — period costumes, unreliable witnesses, red herrings, a butler or two. The Christie estate has been on a strong Netflix run, and *Seven Dials* is arguably the best-looking of the bunch.

**Who it's for:** Fans of *Knives Out*, *Death on the Nile*, or classic British mystery.

## Hot Returning Seasons and Anticipated 2026 Releases

### The Chestnut Man — Season 2

- **Episodes:** 6 (Season 2, subtitled *Hide and Seek*) | **Premieres:** May 7, 2026
- **Rotten Tomatoes:** 100% critics (Season 1)

Five years after its debut, the Danish thriller that defined a certain strain of polished Nordic noir is finally back. Season 1 followed two Copenhagen detectives tracking a killer who left tiny chestnut figurines at each crime scene; Season 2 picks up in the same universe with a new case and a shifted dynamic. With Season 1's perfect critics score still intact, this is the return most Nordic noir fans have been waiting for all year.

### Monster Season 4: The Lizzie Borden Story

- **Episodes:** TBA | **Expected:** Fall 2026
- **Creators:** Ryan Murphy and Ian Brennan

Ryan Murphy's anthology continues with the most famous axe murder case in American history. Ella Beatty stars as Lizzie Borden, with Charlie Hunnam (fresh off playing Ed Gein in Season 3) returning to the franchise as her father Andrew. Vicky Krieps and Rebecca Hall round out the cast. Like every season of *Monster*, expect divisive takes, lush production design, and debate about the ethics of dramatizing real victims.

### Berlin and the Lady with an Ermine

- **Episodes:** 8 | **Premieres:** May 15, 2026

A direct continuation of the *Money Heist* universe, picking up with Berlin (Pedro Alonso) and Damián (Tristán Ulloa) assembling a new crew in Seville for a fake heist that gets them access to a real one: Leonardo da Vinci's *Lady with an Ermine*. More romance and art-world caper than the bank jobs of the original — closer to *Lupin* in tone than *La Casa de Papel*.

### Humint

- **Episodes:** 8 | **Premiered:** March 2026
- **Genre:** Korean spy thriller

A South Korean intelligence agent is sent to Russia to break up a North Korean drug ring and collides with an elite North Korean operative who may or may not be playing a longer game. *Humint* slots neatly into Netflix's growing library of Korean genre prestige — less glossy than *Squid Game*, grimier and more procedural than *The Glory*, with the kind of tight, brutally competent action choreography Korean thrillers are known for.

### The Crash

- **Episodes:** 3 (docuseries) | **Premieres:** May 15, 2026

A three-part documentary on the 2022 case of Ohio teenager Mackenzie Shirilla, who drove into a brick building at 100 mph, killing her boyfriend and his friend. The prosecution argued it was deliberate. The series examines the case, the trial, and the psychological record. Early access critics have flagged it as one of Netflix's more restrained true crime entries of the year.

## International Crime Picks Worth a Flight

### Land of Sin (Swedish)

A Swedish crime thriller that became one of Netflix's top-performing international originals this spring. Viewers compare its tone to *Ozark* — a family trying to hold something criminal together while everything frays — but its pacing and structure are closer to *The Waterfront*. Moody, snowy, and morally grey.

### The Åre Murders (Swedish)

Stockholm detective Hanna Ahlander is sent to a snowed-in ski resort to investigate a missing teenage girl and ends up working alongside a local officer who knows everyone in town. Based on Viveca Sten's novels, this is comfort-food Nordic noir — beautifully shot, methodical, and resolved within a tight limited-series arc.

### The Art of Sarah (Korean)

- **Premiered:** February 13, 2026

A genre-bending Korean thriller built around a beauty-company CEO named Sarah Kim who seems to have multiple identities — and the detective who becomes obsessed with proving it. Part identity thriller, part techno-noir, part corporate mystery. A great pick for viewers who loved *Anna* on Apple TV+ or *The Talented Mr. Ripley*.

### Lupin

- **Episodes:** Part 1-3 available, Part 4 announced
- **Language:** French

Omar Sy's gentleman-thief Assane Diop remains one of Netflix's most charismatic leads. If somehow you missed Parts 1 through 3, this is peak comfort crime — elegant heists, Parisian locations, a genuinely clever con every episode. A fourth part has been confirmed but not dated.

### Who Is Erin Carter?

Set in the sun-bleached south of Spain, this eight-episode thriller follows a British expat whose carefully constructed new life unravels after a violent supermarket robbery exposes skills she shouldn't have. Fast-paced, stylish, and built for binge-watching. Season 2 has been confirmed.

### Dark (German)

Still the standard. *Dark* is technically a sci-fi mystery, but its crime fabric — missing children, a small-town cover-up, generational secrets — makes it essential for crime fans with patience. Viewers consistently rank it among the smartest shows Netflix has ever produced. Three seasons, 26 episodes, one definitive ending.

### How to Get to Heaven from Belfast

A Northern Irish crime drama that weaves three storylines — a woman returning home after her brother's death, a young girl drawn into paramilitary crime, a cop working his last major case — against the raw backdrop of post-Troubles Belfast. More about place, trauma, and identity than any single investigation. Performances are exceptional.

## Modern Classics Still Delivering

### Breaking Bad

- **Episodes:** 62 across 5 seasons
- **IMDb:** 9.5/10 | **Rotten Tomatoes:** 96% critics / 96% audience

No list is complete without it. Walter White's transformation from chemistry teacher to meth kingpin is still the benchmark for long-form character transformation in television. Every season ratchets up the tension without sacrificing character. If you have somehow not watched, 2026 is as good a year as any.

### Better Call Saul

- **Episodes:** 63 across 6 seasons
- **IMDb:** 9.0/10 | **Rotten Tomatoes:** 98% critics

Many viewers argue the *Breaking Bad* prequel is even better than its parent show. Slower, sadder, more about the small choices that make a person irredeemable. Bob Odenkirk's Jimmy McGill is one of the great tragic figures of 21st-century TV.

### Ozark

- **Episodes:** 44 across 4 seasons
- **IMDb:** 8.5/10 | **Rotten Tomatoes:** 82% critics

Jason Bateman as a financial planner laundering money for a cartel in the Missouri Ozarks. It is grim, tightly wound, and held together by one of the most terrifying performances of the decade — Julia Garner as Ruth Langmore. Complete four-season arc with a satisfying ending, which is rare on Netflix.

### Mindhunter

- **Episodes:** 19 across 2 seasons
- **IMDb:** 8.6/10 | **Rotten Tomatoes:** 97% critics

David Fincher's procedural about the FBI's Behavioral Science Unit and the young agents who pioneered criminal profiling by interviewing imprisoned serial killers. Cerebral, methodical, and genuinely unsettling. As of 2026, Season 3 remains unconfirmed — Fincher has expressed interest but not commitment. The two existing seasons stand up perfectly as a complete viewing experience.

### The Night Agent

- **Episodes:** 20 across 2 seasons (Season 3 confirmed)
- **Notable:** One of the most-watched English-language Netflix series of all time

Technically a political thriller more than a crime procedural, but the investigative and conspiracy elements put it in the same neighborhood. Gabriel Basso stars as Peter Sutherland, a low-ranking FBI agent who answers a phone call that pulls him into a deepening White House conspiracy. Pacy, pulpy, and designed for binge consumption.

### Narcos and Narcos: Mexico

- **Episodes:** 60+ combined
- **IMDb:** 8.8/10 (original) / 8.4/10 (*Mexico*)

A sprawling cartel saga that starts with Pablo Escobar's rise in Colombia and jumps to Mexico for the second half of its run. Dense, well-researched, and anchored by a rotating cast of standout performances (Wagner Moura, Pedro Pascal, Diego Luna). Still the gold standard for Netflix crime epics.

## True Crime Docuseries Worth Your Time

### Making a Murderer

The docuseries that arguably created the modern Netflix true crime genre. Steven Avery, Brendan Dassey, and a case that raises uncomfortable questions about the American justice system. Across two parts, it is still one of the most-discussed pieces of streaming television ever made.

### American Nightmare

A three-episode limited docuseries about a 2015 California couple whose kidnapping report was dismissed by police as a hoax — and who were then publicly accused of staging the crime. The real story, and the real perpetrator, will genuinely shock viewers who go in blind. A masterclass in how the genre can expose institutional failure without exploiting its subjects.

### The Gilgo Beach Murders

Three episodes on one of the most chilling unsolved cases in American history, culminating in the 2023 arrest of Rex Heuermann nearly 15 years after the murders. The series includes the haunting 911 calls that kicked off the investigation and a clear-eyed look at how law enforcement failures let the case go cold for over a decade.

### The Tylenol Murders

A three-part docuseries on the 1982 poisonings that killed seven Chicagoans after they took Extra-Strength Tylenol capsules laced with potassium cyanide. The case was never solved, and the series walks through the prime suspects while showing how the tragedy permanently changed the way consumer products are packaged.

### Kidnapped: Elizabeth Smart

A 2026 documentary in which Elizabeth Smart — kidnapped from her Salt Lake City bedroom in 2002 at age 14 and held for nine months — revisits her story in her own words. Less focused on the crime itself than on the psychology of survival and reintegration.

### The Gabby Petito Story

A three-episode documentary on the 2021 case that captivated social media, told through interviews with Petito's family and friends. Heartbreaking, carefully made, and explicit about the domestic-violence warning signs that preceded the murder.

### A Friend, a Murderer

A Danish docuseries released in March 2026, in which three friends revisit a series of crimes that reshaped their lives in rural Denmark. Quietly devastating, and a good pick for viewers who find the big-swing American docuseries exhausting.

## Hidden Gems That Deserve More Attention

### Veronica Mars

A teen detective series that still holds up 20 years later. Kristen Bell plays a high school P.I. in a class-stratified California beach town, balancing mystery-of-the-week cases with overarching season-long investigations. Sharp writing, real emotional depth, and genuinely funny.

### Bodyguard

A six-episode British thriller that launched Richard Madden's post-*Game of Thrones* career. He plays a war veteran assigned to protect a controversial Home Secretary. The opening train sequence alone is worth the subscription. Tight, tense, and contained.

### The Stranger

One of the earlier Harlan Coben adaptations and still one of the best. Richard Armitage plays a man whose ordinary suburban life is shattered when a stranger shows up with a devastating secret about his wife. Eight episodes, one clean resolution.

### Fool Me Once

More Coben, this time with Michelle Keegan as a former military pilot who sees her murdered husband on a nanny cam a week after his funeral. One of Netflix's most-watched limited series of 2024 for good reason — it is completely unapologetic about being a propulsive, twisty binge.

### Missing You

The most recent Coben adaptation, released in early 2025 and still a consistent library performer. A detective spots her long-missing fiancé on a dating app 11 years after he vanished. Five episodes, and the payoff is actually earned.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best crime show on Netflix right now in 2026?

As of April 2026, the most-discussed recent releases are *His & Hers* (a twisty limited series that hit number one in January), *Jo Nesbø's Detective Hole* (a Norwegian noir that launched in March), and *That Night* (a Spanish-language limited thriller that became a global top-ten hit within days). For all-time favorites, *Breaking Bad* consistently leads critic and audience rankings with its 9.5 IMDb score. Netflix's Top 10 shifts weekly — any of these is a safe landing spot.

### What are the best true crime documentaries on Netflix in 2026?

The standout 2026 true crime releases are *Trust Me: The False Prophet* (four parts, 100% critics score on Rotten Tomatoes), *Kidnapped: Elizabeth Smart*, and *A Friend, a Murderer*. The upcoming *The Crash*, set to premiere in May 2026, has generated significant early buzz. From Netflix's library, *Making a Murderer*, *American Nightmare*, and *The Gilgo Beach Murders* are the most frequently recommended by viewers.

### Is His & Hers based on a true story?

No. *His & Hers* is adapted from Alice Feeney's 2020 novel of the same name. It's fiction, but the novel was praised for its grounded portrayal of tabloid culture and police investigation, which the show carries over.

### When does The Chestnut Man Season 2 come out?

*The Chestnut Man* Season 2, subtitled *Hide and Seek*, premieres on Netflix on May 7, 2026. It's the first new season in five years — Season 1 launched in 2021 with a perfect 100% Rotten Tomatoes critics score, which has remained intact.

### When does Monster Season 4: The Lizzie Borden Story come out?

Netflix has not yet confirmed a premiere date for *Monster* Season 4. Filming is wrapping in early 2026, and given that prior seasons have consistently released in late September or early October, a Fall 2026 launch is the working expectation.

### Is Mindhunter getting a Season 3?

As of April 2026, there is still no confirmed Season 3 for *Mindhunter*. David Fincher has publicly expressed interest multiple times but has not committed to production, and cast availability has been cited as a complication. The two existing seasons function well as a complete arc, and remain some of the best crime television Netflix has ever produced.

### What crime shows on Netflix are based on true stories?

Among scripted series: *Mindhunter* (based on real FBI Behavioral Science Unit cases), *Narcos* and *Narcos: Mexico* (Pablo Escobar and the Mexican cartels), and *Monster* (each season fictionalizes a real killer). Among docuseries: *Making a Murderer*, *American Nightmare*, *The Gilgo Beach Murders*, *The Tylenol Murders*, *The Gabby Petito Story*, and *Kidnapped: Elizabeth Smart* are all built directly on real cases.

### Are there any good Korean or Nordic crime shows on Netflix in 2026?

Yes — 2026 has been a strong year for both. Korean standouts include *Humint* (spy thriller) and *The Art of Sarah* (identity thriller). Nordic picks include the returning *The Chestnut Man* Season 2 (Danish), *Jo Nesbø's Detective Hole* (Norwegian), *Land of Sin* (Swedish), and *The Åre Murders* (Swedish). Collectively they represent some of the best non-English crime content currently streaming anywhere.

### Is there a Money Heist spin-off in 2026?

Yes. *Berlin and the Lady with an Ermine* premieres on May 15, 2026. It reunites Pedro Alonso's Berlin with a new crew for an art-focused heist in Seville, continuing the *Money Heist* universe in a slightly lighter, romance-tinged direction.

## The Bottom Line

2026 is shaping up to be one of the strongest years for crime television on Netflix since the original golden run of *Breaking Bad*, *Narcos*, and *Mindhunter*. Between the January-to-April flood of new limited series, the returning Nordic heavyweights in May, and the prestige anthology continuations arriving in the fall, there is genuinely too much to watch — which is a better problem than most streaming services are giving viewers this year.

Pick by mood: a short propulsive binge (*His & Hers*, *That Night*), a meaty weekend project (*Detective Hole*), a true crime deep dive (*Trust Me: The False Prophet*), or a classic that still rewards a rewatch (*Breaking Bad*, *Better Call Saul*, *Ozark*). Whatever direction you go, leave a light on.
